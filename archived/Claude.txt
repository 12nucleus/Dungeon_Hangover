# DUNGEON HANGOVER: 50 FLOORS OF REGRET
### Story & Content Design Document

**Numbering convention:** Greg wakes at the bottom, **Floor 50**, and climbs toward the exit, **Floor 1**, at street level. "Up" always means "lower floor number." This doc uses that convention throughout.

---

## PART 1 — THE OVERARCHING STORYLINE

### The Hook
Greg passed out mid-bar-fight after his tenth (or was it eleventh?) shot of something called "Regret." He wakes up at the bottom of a dungeon that, as he descends deeper into memory and climbs higher toward the exit, turns out to be a physical manifestation of *the worst night of his life*, built and enforced by a cosmic bureaucracy of hangover management: **The Tabkeeper's Ledger**, an afterlife-adjacent office that processes drunks who did something stupid enough to warrant "processing" before they're allowed to either wake up or move on. Greg isn't dead. He's *overdue*.

The dungeon is called **The Last Call**, and every floor down represents an earlier, worse decision of the night; every floor up is Greg sobering up, literally and narratively, one skill point at a time.

### The Five Acts

| Act | Floors | Name | Theme | Antagonist Faction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 50–41 | **The Bilge** | Sewers, cellars, rat-infested storage beneath the bar. Filth, vermin, goblins squatting in the wreckage. | The Goblin King's court (goblins who've made a slum kingdom in the flooded sub-basements) |
| II | 40–31 | **The Tap Room Wars** | A frozen-in-time recreation of the bar itself, mid-brawl, looping the fight that got Greg thrown down here in the first place. | Rival bar patrons turned undead brawlers, led by **Knuckles McGraw**, the bouncer Greg supposedly sucker-punched |
| III | 30–21 | **The Hazy Mezzanine** | Upper floor of the bar, warped into a hallucinatory funhouse of mirrors, karaoke stages, and repeating hours. Reality gets unreliable. | The **Chorus of Last Calls**, spectral karaoke hosts who trap patrons in encore loops |
| IV | 20–11 | **Management** | Corporate-purgatory floor: the Ledger's actual back office, all filing cabinets, tab contracts, and debt collectors. | **The Auditors**, bureaucratic demons who want Greg to sign over his liver (literally, as collateral) |
| V | 10–1 | **Last Call** | The street outside the bar, dawn breaking, reality re-stabilizing. Final confrontation with the Tabkeeper. | **The Tabkeeper** themself |

### Major Beats (boss floors and turning points)

- **Floor 50 (start):** Wake up in the flooded sub-basement. Recruit the Hermit. Learn the Goblin King has taken over the old bath-house cellar. *(Full breakdown in Part 4.)*
- **Floor 46:** First bonfire hub with a proper vendor — a drowned bartender ghost named **Gill** who only trades in gossip and bottle caps.
- **Floor 44:** Companion questline resolves (or doesn't) — see **Sister Mercy**, below.
- **Floor 41 (Act I boss):** **The Goblin King, Bloathelm III** — reintroduced properly if the player only fought his bath-guard stand-in on Floor 50; here he returns for real with his court, having fled floor 50 with Greg's stolen boots.
- **Floor 31 (Act II boss):** **Knuckles McGraw**, the undead bouncer, in a bar-brawl arena where the whole room is a weapon.
- **Floor 21 (Act III boss):** **The Encore**, a hallucinated fusion of every karaoke host Greg embarrassed himself in front of, fought across a stage that keeps replaying his cringiest moments as literal hazards.
- **Floor 11 (Act IV boss):** **The Head Auditor**, a demon in a cheap suit who fights with paperwork given form (contracts as whips, staple-gun crossbows).
- **Floor 1 (final):** **The Tabkeeper**, revealed as the bartender who served Greg his last drink. No health bar in the traditional sense — the fight is a dialogue-and-combat hybrid where Greg's choices (skills leveled, companions kept, quests resolved) determine which of three endings triggers.

### The Ending(s)
At Floor 1, dawn is breaking over the street outside the bar. The Tabkeeper offers Greg the actual tab for the night — and how Greg "pays" depends on the run:

1. **Sober Ending — "Closing Time":** If Greg resolved most companion arcs and kept his Blessed uptime high, the Tabkeeper simply lets him walk out. Cut to Greg blinking in the sunlight, hungover but alive, dagger still tucked in his waistband as a souvenir. Roll credits over a montage of every ridiculous item he's still, inexplicably, carrying.
2. **Regular Ending — "One For the Road":** A middle outcome — Greg is allowed to leave but the Tabkeeper keeps something of his (a memory, a name, a companion's number scrawled on a napkin that mysteriously blurs). Bittersweet, implies a sequel hook.
3. **Relapse Ending — "Last Call, Again":** If the player burned every companion and racked up Cursed stacks instead of Blessed, the Tabkeeper pours Greg one more drink "on the house," and the last shot of the game is Greg, tempted, reaching for the glass as the screen fades — dungeon reset teased for New Game+.

## PART 2 — THE 15 CLASSES

There is no class-select screen. These are the emergent archetype pools skills are drawn from at level-up. A "class" is just what a player's build looks like after the fact.

## PART 4 — FLOOR 50 DETAILED BUILD (Player's First Floor)

### Overview
Floor 50 is the flooded sub-basement beneath the bar — a wrecked storage cellar the Goblin King's court has half-claimed as slum territory, complete with a stolen bath-house fixture they use as a throne room. 25 rooms, one bonfire at the start, a second emergency bonfire near the midpoint, ending in a boss fight against a goblin sentry-turned-encounter and the Goblin King himself.

### Opening Sequence
Greg wakes face-down on wet stone, in his underwear, next to a guttering bonfire. A torch and starter bag (rusty dagger, health potion) sit beside him. Standing triggers the "hangover stand-up" animation — a few star-particle effects circling his head.

> **NARRATOR (text bark):** "And there he is. Ladies and gentlemen, Greg. Last night he punched a man named Knuckles, tried to marry a bar stool, and — this part's my favorite — is now waking up fifty floors underground in his boxers. Let's see how far pride carries a man with no pants."

---

### Room-by-Room Breakdown

**R1 — Bonfire Landing (start).** The bonfire checkpoint. A cracked mirror shard on the wall lets the player see Greg's underwear-clad reflection (comedic beat, optional narrator line). Exit north to R2.

**R2 — The Hermit's Alcove.** A grizzled old drunk, **Hermit Alvin**, squats amid candle stubs and empty bottles. He gives the opening quest.

> **ALVIN:** "You're new. Or you're dead. Either way — you got hands? Good, I need 'em. That damn rat took my finger. My *whole* finger. Wedding ring still on it, if you can believe the nerve."
>
> **GREG (dialogue option):** "Why don't you just... get a new ring?"
>
> **ALVIN:** "It's not about the ring, boy, it's about the *principle*. Also yes the ring, it's worth a fortune, my late wife had taste. Bring it back and I'll set you straight on where the hell you are."

Quest: **The Hermit's Finger.** Objective updates once the Boss Rat is found (R17). Reward on completion: starter gear pack (see loot table) + full storyline exposition (Alvin explains the Tabkeeper's Ledger, the bar-fight, why Greg's here).

**R3 — Rat Warren Entrance.** Narrow tunnel, 2 basic rat enemies, introduces Bleeding status via rat-bite attacks.

**R4 — Gnawed Bone Pile.** Environmental room: a pile of debris can be shoved to reveal a shortcut to R16. Minor loot cache.

**R5 — Boss Rat's Larder.** Empty food stash guarded by 3 rats; narrator comments on the smell. Clue item here (a chewed ring box) confirms the Boss Rat's lair is further in.

**R6 — Collapsed Cellar.** Puzzle room: a barrel of oil can be shoved down a slope to clear rubble blocking R7, or ignited later for a Fry Cook shortcut once that skill is unlocked (replay-value nod).

**R7 — Sewage Grate Junction.** Hub room, three exits (R8, R9, R10). Ankle-deep water — first taste of "Slippery" terrain interactions.

**R8 — Drowned Barkeep's Corpse.** Lore room. A dead patron floats here with a journal (world-building flavor text) and a small gold cache.

**R9 — Fungus Grove.** Glowing fungus enemies introduce the **Poisoned** status effect. 2–3 fungal enemies.

**R10 — Old Wash Room.** Broken soap dispensers, dry fountains. First hint of the soap subplot — a faded sign: *"Property of the Royal Bath — Do Not Remove Suds."* Empty soap dish. Narrator mocks the very concept of a goblin king with a skincare routine.

**R11 — Goblin Laundry Den.** 3–4 goblin enemies guarding a locked chest. Killing them or sneaking past (Gutter Rogue-friendly room) gets the chest key.

**R12 — Soap Vault.** The locked chest room. Contains **The King's Own Bar of Suds** (quest item) plus a floor trap (tripwire dumps a bucket — slapstick, minor Slippery debuff on the player, purely comedic).

**R13 — Bathhouse Antechamber.** Companion recruitment room. **Sister Mercy**, the disgraced Karaoke Bard, is chained here awaiting "processing" by the goblins. Freeing her (fight or persuade) recruits her for the rest of Phase 1's slice.

> **SISTER MERCY:** "Don't just stand there gawking, prop — either cut me loose or hand me a mic, one of the two is getting me out of this chair."

**R14 — Flooded Cistern.** Set-piece encounter: a reanimated fish-skeleton enemy. This is the tone-reference slapstick fight — narrator explicitly comments if the player kills it using the starter dagger vs. improvising with an actual fish object floating in the water.

> **NARRATOR:** "A skeleton. Made of fish bones. Fighting you with what appears to be... another fish. This is the loneliest sentence I've ever had to narrate."

**R15 — The Gambler's Nook.** Optional side room: a goblin gambler runs a dice mini-game for bonus gold, unconnected to main critical path.

**R16 — Broken Stairs Landing.** Second bonfire (mid-floor checkpoint). Shortcut back to R4 via the shoved debris pile.

**R17 — Rat King's Shrine.** **Boss Rat (Grabknuckle)** mini-boss fight — a larger rat wearing the Hermit's ring like a crude crown-nose-piercing. On defeat, drops the ring; completes *The Hermit's Finger* when returned to Alvin.

**R18 — Trophy Hall.** Goblin King's trophy room — stolen bar stools, a dartboard, Greg's missing pants (Floor 49 quest tease, technically found one floor early as an easter egg). Lore flavor text on the Goblin King's rise to power.

**R19 — Steam Tunnels.** Environmental hazard room: scalding steam vents that can be shoved/ignited to hit enemies; introduces the idea that combat and exploration share physics (Pillar 4).

**R20 — Guard Barracks.** 4-goblin patrol fight, harder than R11's den. Best cleared with the Sister Mercy companion active.

**R21 — Locked Door Foyer.** The door to the Royal Bath. A **Goblin Guard** stands post.

> **GOBLIN GUARD:** "Oi. Nobody sees His Grace mid-soak without proper hygiene clearance. You got soap?"
>
> *(If player lacks The King's Own Bar of Suds:)*

**R22 — Bath Supply Closet.** Backup soap room in case R12 was skipped/missed — a lesser-quality "Off-Brand Suds" bar sits here as a fallback (works for the guard, but has a worse item-effect if equipped/used as a throwable).

**R23 — The Boiler Room.** Optional side room housing a captive Sommelier NPC still-brewer, teaching the player their first Sommelier recipe.

**R24 — Antechamber to the Bath.** Final save/prep room — a bonfire-adjacent respawn anchor specifically for the boss attempt, plus a vendor-goblin (defected, comedic) selling last-minute items.

**R25 — The Royal Bath (Boss Room).** **Goblin King Bloathelm III**, mid-bath, is interrupted.

> **BLOATHELM III:** *(sitting in a chipped claw-foot tub, wearing a rubber-duck crown)* "DO YOU KNOCK?! I am NAKED and RELAXING and — is that the Hermit's ring? You've been in MY warren?! GUARDS! ...oh right, you killed the guards. Fine. FINE. We do this the wet way."

Boss fight: Bloathelm fights from and around the tub — splashing AOE water attacks (can extinguish player fire effects, notably countering Fry Cook builds — a deliberate environmental gimmick), throws bath toys as projectiles, and can shove the tub itself as a heavy Environment object. On defeat, he flees (setting up his Floor 41 return) but drops his signature club.

### The Final Boss Weapon

**Scrubnuckle, the Royal Bath Cudgel**
*A dense, waterlogged oak club, still faintly bubbled with suds, with a rubber duck crudely nailed to the pommel as a counterweight. It has clearly been used to both crush skulls and scrub a very large royal back, sometimes in the same evening.*

- **Type:** Two-handed Great Club (Martial pool, Bar Bouncer/Bar Back synergy)
- **Base Effect:** High blunt damage, slow (reduced initiative)
- **Passive — "Squeaky Clean":** 20% chance on hit to apply **Slippery** to the target (reduces their Accuracy and Evasion for 2 turns)
- **Passive — "Royally Scented":** User gains a small resistance to Poisoned (goblin filth doesn't stick to something this clean) but is also permanently flagged with the flavor tag *Always Smells Like Lavender*, which the narrator will not let go of for the rest of the game
- **Side Effect:** Occasionally "leaks" a trail of soap bubbles while running (visual only, zero mechanical effect, pure Pillar-4-approved absurdity)
- **Flavor line (on pickup):** *"You have acquired Scrubnuckle. It is somehow both a warclub and a back-scratcher. You are unreasonably proud of this."*
